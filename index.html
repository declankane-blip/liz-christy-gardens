While you’re here

Enjoy the garden.

Sunbathe, read, bird watch, stroll, relax, listen, and smell. A few requests keep it lovely for everyone.

Leave the flowers
Please don’t pick or cut flowers or plants. If one catches your eye, ask a gardener its name and look for it at a Greenmarket.
Stay on the paths
Don’t step into a plot without its gardener’s permission. Dogs stay on the paths too.
Children are welcome
Kids can visit with their parents and learn to garden. Running and wild play aren’t allowed.
Let the pond be
Please don’t add fish or turtles. The pond can only safely support its current population.
Handheld cameras only
Tripods and other equipment block the paths, so they aren’t permitted.
Ask us anything
Gardeners are glad to talk about plants, and to hear what you know.
History

Fifty years on one corner.

How a rubble-strewn lot became New York City’s first community garden.

History

From Stuyvesant’s farm to a protected garden.

By the 1970s this corner was a vacant lot in a neighborhood of abandoned buildings. A local resident named Liz Christy saw a garden in it.

  1. 1600s

    A Dutch bouwerie

    The corner of Bouwerie and North Street was the southern tip of a farm owned by Peter Stuyvesant, the last Dutch Governor of New Amsterdam. Bouwerie is Dutch for farm.

  2. 1973

    The Green Guerillas move in

    Liz Christy and a group of gardening activists were planting window boxes, tree pits, and vacant lots with seed bombs. In December they went to the City for official use of the lot. Volunteers hauled out garbage and rubble, spread donated topsoil, put up a fence, and started planting.

  3. 1974

    The $1 lease

    On April 23, the City’s office of Housing Preservation and Development approved the lot for rent. Sixty raised beds were planted with vegetables, followed by trees and herbaceous borders.

  4. 1975

    A model for the city

    In its second year the garden won its first Mollie Parnis Dress Up Your Neighborhood Award. People from all five boroughs asked how to start their own. The Green Guerillas ran workshops, tested plants in hostile conditions, and gave plants away to new gardens across the City.

  5. 1986

    Named for its founder

    The garden was dedicated as Liz Christy’s Bowery-Houston Garden, in her memory.

  6. 1990

    A pledge to keep it whole

    After years of uncertainty and a groundswell of support, the Cooper Square Committee pledged to preserve the entire garden in its renovation plans for the neighborhood.

  7. 2002

    Protected

    An agreement between the City of New York and the New York State Attorney General calls for the preservation of the Liz Christy Garden.

The neighborhood today

Still full of color.

The blocks around Bowery and Houston that were in decline in the 1970s are lively again, painted walls and all.

Volunteer

Grow with us.

Every member of the garden started as a volunteer. Whether you’ve gardened for decades or never planted a seed, just come during open hours.

Your first time

How to start volunteering.

When
During open hours: Saturdays, noon to 4 PM, all year. From May through September, also Sundays noon to 4 PM and Tuesday and Thursday from 6 PM to dusk.
Where
The garden gate on Houston Street, between the Bowery and Second Avenue.
What to do
Introduce yourself to the gardener on duty and say you’d like to volunteer. They’ll find you something to do and log your hours.
After your first visit

From volunteer to member.

What matters is your desire to garden. Beginners learn alongside seasoned gardeners, and experienced hands are welcome to share what they know. Members design and tend their own areas of the garden, and everyone shares the general upkeep.

  1. Come during open hours and introduce yourself to the gardener on duty.
  2. They’ll give you work and log your hours in the book kept in the shed.
  3. After 20 hours, you’re eligible for a key to the garden.
  4. After 40 hours, you’re a Gardener with voting rights.

How to give

Two ways to donate.

  1. In the garden

    Drop cash or a check in the collection basket, or hand a check to the gardener on duty. The basket is out during open hours: Saturdays noon to 4 PM all year, plus Sundays noon to 4 PM and Tuesday and Thursday evenings from 6 PM to dusk, May through September.

  2. By mail, any time

    Mail a check made out to “Green Guerillas” with “Liz Christy” on the memo line. The memo line makes sure your gift goes to this garden.
